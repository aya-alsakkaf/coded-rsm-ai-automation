Designing AI Agents for Real Work

Identify and build a working AI agent that replaces a real task from daily work. Find the task that eats your week. Design an agent for it. Build it in Claude.

Day brief

Day 1 starts with one KYC pack and two instructions. Then: how an AI model writes, which tasks are worth automating, the Agent Design Framework (input, instruction, output), and a working agent in Claude that you test and improve.

You build today: Your agent in a Claude Project, version 2, with three test cases.

Your turn

4 hands-on tasks. The timer runs on the main screen. Every prompt is on your prompt sheet.

Your turn · 1.1 · 10 min

Your task list, filtered

  1. Write 5 tasks from your last two weeks.
  2. Score each task with the four questions.
  3. Circle your top 3. Share one with your table.
10:00 on the clock · PAPER OR CLAUDE
What to look for: Three candidate tasks, each with four yes or no answers.
Your turn · 1.2 · 20 min

Fill one Agent Card

  1. Pick one candidate task from the morning.
  2. Fill the Agent Card: job, input, instruction, output, three tests.
  3. Swap with a partner. Could a new joiner run your card?
20:00 on the clock · AGENT CARD
What to look for: A card where every part is specific: a named file, numbered steps, fixed output columns.
Your turn · 1.3 · 40 min

Build sprint 1: your agent, version 1

  1. Create a Claude Project with your agent's name.
  2. Paste the starter instruction from your prompt sheet into the project instructions.
  3. Add the knowledge file. Start a new chat and attach the case file.
  4. Check three values in the output against the source file.
40:00 on the clock · CLAUDE
What to look for: An output in the exact format your card asks for, with file and page for every fact.

Your turn · 1.4 · 40 min

Build sprint 2: three tests, one fix

  1. Run your three test cases. Write the expected answer first.
  2. Note what failed.
  3. Change one line of the instruction. Run all three tests again.
40:00 on the clock · CLAUDE
What to look for: Version 2 of your instruction and a short test log: case, expected, actual, fixed.
